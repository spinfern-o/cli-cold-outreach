## 2. Structure by recipient

**Named founder or eng manager.** Explain why them, since they can say yes. Give a specific product detail, one proof line, and a small ask. "Who should I talk to?" is a fine fallback ask.

**Shared inbox (careers@, hello@, info@).** Someone triages it and may forward it.
- Put role and timing in the subject: "Summer 2027 intern: CS (Python, PyTorch)".
- Say who you are and what you want in the first two lines.
- Keep it short, but keep the one personalization line, because generic mail gets filtered.
- Ask "Who would be the best person to ask about a Summer 2027 internship?"

## 3. Templates (skeletons; fill only with real facts)

**A. Founder, small team**
> Subject: Summer intern for [Product]?
> Hi [Name], [specific thing about product/feature and what you noticed]. I'm a [year] CS student at [School] and built [project] ([link]), which [concrete result]. That's close to [their problem]. Is a Summer 2027 intern something you'd consider? Larry Wang

**B. CTO / eng manager (technical angle)**
> Subject: Question on [tech/repo/post]
> Hi [Name], I read [post/repo] about [X]. [One sentence on what you tried or noticed.] I built [project] with [same stack], and [how you verified it]. Would your team consider a Summer 2027 intern for work like that?

**C. Shared inbox**
> Subject: Summer 2027 intern: CS ([stack])
> Hi [Company] team, I'm Larry Wang, a CS student at Michigan looking for a Summer 2027 engineering internship. [One specific line about their product.] I built [project] ([link]) in [stack]. Who would be the right person to ask?

**D. Contribution-led (you've touched their product/open source)**
> Subject: [PR/issue] on [repo]
> Hi [Name], I opened [PR/issue] fixing [thing]. I'd like to do more work like that as a Summer 2027 intern. Is that something your team takes on?

## 4. Subject lines

Work:
- short and specific, 3–6 words
- "Question on [feature]"
- role and timing, for shared inboxes
- a real mutual referral

Hurt:
- "Internship application" or "Opportunity"
- ALL CAPS, emojis
- fake "Re:" or "Fwd:"
- anything that gets cut off on mobile
- gimmicks

## 5. Mistakes that kill replies

- flattery with no specifics
- filler openers
- an attachment in the first email (link instead) **[opinion]**
- several asks
- more than ~125 words
- overclaiming ("expert in AI")
- identical mass emails
- emailing a founder at a 300-person company
- sending at night or on weekends
- never following up

## 6. Follow-ups

1–2 follow-ups, 3–7 days apart, in the same thread, 2–3 lines each. Add something new: an update, a link, a fix. Give an easy out.

> Hi Priya, following up in case this got buried. This week I added offline conflict tests to syncnotes (link). If now isn't a good time, is there someone else I should ask? Larry
